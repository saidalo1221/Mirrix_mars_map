# Imagery verification

Olympus, Valles, and Elysium each use a 1,536 × 1,536 CTX context mosaic assembled from level 11 tiles at about 10 m equatorial pixel spacing. Tile rows and columns are 826–828 and 530–532 for Olympus, 1154–1156 and 1254–1256 for Valles, and 998–1000 and 3837–3839 for Elysium. Each mosaic surrounds the separate HiRISE elevation window. [Murray Lab CTX service](https://astro.arcgis.com/arcgis/rest/services/OnMars/CTX/MapServer)

Mawrth Vallis and Gusev each have a 1,536 × 1,536 CTX mosaic at approximately 10 m equatorial pixel spacing. Both use nine level 11 tiles from the [Caltech Murray Lab CTX service](https://astro.arcgis.com/arcgis/rest/services/OnMars/CTX/MapServer). Mawrth uses rows 770–772 and columns 1837–1839. Gusev uses rows 1188–1190 and columns 4043–4045. The mosaics surround the bundled HiRISE elevation windows and supply texture to their 3D relief. The viewer blends their edges into the surrounding imagery. The dashed boundary marks the detailed elevation and routing footprint. Some registration offset between the CTX imagery and the HiRISE elevations is possible.

Jezero and Gale use local CTX context imagery around the numerical elevation footprint. Each 1536 × 1536 mosaic stitches a three-by-three block of verified NASA/JPL/MSSS/Caltech Murray Lab beta01 CTX tiles from Esri's Mars 2000 geographic service. Jezero uses level 10, rows 406–408 and columns 1463–1465, at about 20 m per pixel. Gale uses level 11, rows 1076–1078 and columns 3609–3611, at about 10 m per pixel. This preliminary imagery may have registration offsets relative to the elevation grids. It provides visual context and does not extend measured relief or routing. Outside the context mosaics, the renderer falls back to the selected global Mars image. [CTX service](https://astro.arcgis.com/arcgis/rest/services/OnMars/CTX/MapServer), [Murray Lab dataset](https://murray-lab.caltech.edu/CTX/beta01.html)

The current Jezero optical overlay uses 16 verified HiRISE level 12 PNGs, rows 1626–1629 and columns 5856–5859. The 1024 × 1024 mosaic retains the original regional bounds listed below and delivers about 10 m equatorial pixel spacing. The terrain height source remains the 20 m CTX DEM.

NASA Mars Trek GetCapabilities returned HTTP 200 for each of the four requested layer IDs on November 14, 2026. The equatorial services advertised `default028mm` with CRS `urn:ogc:def:crs:EPSG::104905`, a northwest origin at 180°W, 90°N, and 256 × 256 pixel tiles. In this matrix set, level 0 uses two columns and one row. The matrix width and height double per level.

The dashboard bundles two versions of the NASA/JPL/USGS Viking MDIM 2.1 color mosaic. The default image is a 4,096 × 2,048 resample of an 8,536 × 4,268 public archival copy of the [USGS Viking product](https://astrogeology.usgs.gov/search/details/Mars/Viking/MDIM21/Mars_Viking_MDIM21_ClrMosaic_global_232m/cub). The [Planetary Society archival file](https://planetary.s3.amazonaws.com/assets/images/4-mars/2014/Mars_Viking_MDIM21_ClrMosaic_global_2500m.png) supplied those pixels. The Mars Trek layer stitches all eight verified WMTS level-one tiles into a 1,024 × 512 JPEG. Both use signed east longitude, north-up rows, and a 2:1 full-planet equirectangular footprint.

The separate MOLA shaded relief layer is generated from the numerical PDS grid. Its color encodes elevation and its hillshade is illustrative. The preprocessing script `scripts/build-mola-relief.py` regenerates it using 5× display slope, 315° illumination azimuth, and 44° illumination altitude. This visual layer is separate from the Mars Trek MOLA color shaded relief product.

The optional Jezero overlay stitches four HiRISE level 11 PNGs, columns 2928–2929 and rows 813–814. The bounds are 77.34375–77.51953125°E and 18.369140625–18.544921875°N. Its 512 × 512 raster has about 20 m pixels at the equator, coarser than the underlying 0.25 m imagery product. The renderer derives UVs from the mosaic's geographic footprint. This overlay supplies image texture and the CTX DEM supplies the geometry.

Jezero's daytime thermal infrared layer uses the original 256 × 256 Mars Trek `default028mm` PNG at level 9, row 203, column 732. It covers 77.34375–77.6953125°E and 18.28125–18.6328125°N. Its delivered pixel spacing is about 81 m at the equator from the tile geometry. The source controlled mosaic is approximately 100 m/pixel. The verification checked that the complete Jezero DEM footprint fell inside the optical and thermal bounds and that the decoded thermal pixels over the DEM were opaque with varying grayscale intensity. The tile SHA-256 is `6bd5b939669b9693991d53c366f58174feb8b270521f3f8c0a26db09bc55c340`. The stretched grayscale display has no temperature calibration in the app. Gale's regional thermal overlay is unavailable. [Source tile](https://trek.nasa.gov/tiles/Mars/EQ/THEMIS_DayIR_ControlledMosaics_100m_v2_oct2018/1.0.0/default/default028mm/9/203/732.png), [USGS product documentation](https://stac.astrogeology.usgs.gov/docs/data/mars/themis_controlled_mosaics/)
